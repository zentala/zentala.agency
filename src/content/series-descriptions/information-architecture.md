Organizing, labelling and structuring information is the oldest discipline in
this stack and the least respected one. It decides whether a new person is
productive in a week or a quarter, whether a decision made two years ago can
still be found, and — now — whether an AI agent can work in your codebase at
all.

This series covers the craft and its consequences: taxonomy, ontology,
metadata and navigation as engineering concerns rather than librarian
hobbies; documentation rewritten for two readers at once, because the next
developer and the next agent want exactly the same thing from it; and
onboarding treated as a continuous, asynchronous process rather than a first
week. Nobody is ever finished being onboarded — not the new hire, not the
five-year veteran hitting an unfamiliar service, and certainly not the agent
that starts every session with an empty head.

Design for that and you pay once for two wins. Skip it and no amount of AI
budget compensates: a model given unstructured knowledge fails the same way a
person does, only faster and more confidently.
