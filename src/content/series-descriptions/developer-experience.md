Developer Experience is not a tooling budget. It is the answer to a simple
question: how much of a developer's day is spent on the problem they were
hired to solve, and how much on everything around it — finding the service,
finding the owner, finding the doc, waiting for a pipeline, guessing at a
convention nobody wrote down.

This series is the practitioner's side of that: why DevEx pays for itself,
why an internal developer portal is the usual first move, how to start a
Backstage rollout so it survives contact with a real organization, and why
the hard part is never the software. Teams do not resist better tools. They
resist tools imposed on them, and the difference between the two decides
whether the platform gets used or gets worked around.

The last part turns the corner: the same catalog, ownership data and
templates that were built to orient a new hire turn out to be exactly what an
AI agent needs to do real work. The portal you built for humans is the
interface your agents were missing.
